The Fusha Arabic alphabet has 28 letters, written right to left. Most letters change shape depending on their position in a word — isolated, initial, medial, or final — but each keeps a recognisable core. Short vowels are written as marks above and below the letters rather than as letters. Most learners recognise all 28 within two to three weeks.

## How letters connect

Arabic is always cursive — letters join up, even in print. Each letter has up to four forms:

- **Isolated** — standing alone
- **Initial** — at the start of a connected group
- **Medial** — joined on both sides
- **Final** — at the end, joined on the right

Six letters (ا د ذ ر ز و) never connect to the letter *after* them. These "non-connectors" are what let you break an unfamiliar word into readable chunks.

## Short vowels (tashkeel)

The alphabet above covers consonants and long vowels. Short vowels are diacritical marks:

- **fatha** ( ـَ ) — short *a*
- **kasra** ( ـِ ) — short *i*
- **damma** ( ـُ ) — short *u*
- **sukoon** ( ـْ ) — no vowel
- **shadda** ( ـّ ) — doubles the consonant

Everyday Arabic text omits these entirely. Beginner material, children's books, and the Qur'an keep them. Learn with the marks, then wean off gradually.

## A practical learning order

Do not learn the alphabet alphabetically. Learn it by shape family, because lookalike letters are the ones you will confuse:

1. **ب ت ث** — same skeleton, one, two, and three dots
2. **ج ح خ** — same skeleton, dot below / none / above
3. **د ذ** and **ر ز** — the non-connectors
4. **س ش** and **ص ض** — sibilants and their emphatic pairs
5. **ط ظ** and **ع غ** — the hardest pairs for English speakers
6. **ف ق** and **ك ل م ن** — high-frequency, easy shapes
7. **ه و ي ا** — the vowel carriers

Then immediately start reading real words. Recognising *kitaab* (book) is worth more than reciting the alphabet in order.

## How long it takes

| Stage | Typical time |
|---|---|
| Recognise all 28 isolated letters | 1–2 weeks |
| Read fully vowelled words out loud | 3–6 weeks |
| Read short vowelled texts fluently | 2–3 months |
| Read unvowelled text comfortably | 12–18 months |

## FAQ

### Is the Fusha alphabet different from the Gulf or Egyptian alphabet?

No. All Arabic varieties use the same 28-letter alphabet. What differs between dialects is pronunciation and vocabulary, not the script.

### Do I need to learn to handwrite Arabic?

Handwriting is optional, but writing letters by hand significantly speeds up recognition. Even a few weeks of tracing pays off in reading speed.

### Are there capital letters in Arabic?

No. Arabic has no upper and lower case, which removes an entire layer of complexity compared with European alphabets.

### Why do some words look completely different when written?

Because of positional forms and ligatures — لا (laam-alif) is the most common. Once you know the four positional shapes, these stop being surprising.
